# LieSeries

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/1469845757.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24547043)

A Wolfram Language (Mathematica) package for computing with **Lie series in the free Lie algebra on two generators**, expressed in **generalized Hall bases** in the sense of Viennot.

It provides:

- Lie brackets of truncated Lie series with symbolic (polynomial, rational, parameter-dependent) coefficients;
- coefficients in a choice of Hall bases (classical Hall-type orders, Lyndon basis, and others);
- substitution of the generators by other Lie series, by matrices, or by any objects with a user-supplied bracket (e.g. vector fields);
- the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff series log(e^X e^Y), its symmetric version log(e^{X/2} e^Y e^{X/2}), exp(ad_A)B, and the Zassenhaus terms.

## Mathematical setting

Let L(X, Y) be the free Lie algebra on two generators, graded by degree. A *generalized Hall set* (Viennot) is a totally ordered set H of binary trees with leaves X, Y such that the corresponding nested brackets form a basis of L(X, Y). The trees in H depend on the chosen order. The package builds H degree by degree up to a maximal degree N. The built-in orders are listed by `HallOrders[]`:

| `HallOrder` | Description |
|---|---|
| `"Standard"` (default) | trees compared by construction index (degree first, then order of generation) |
| `"Lyndon"` | Lyndon basis (lexicographic order of foliage words) |
| `"Tall"` | trees compared by "tall degree", then index |
| `"Left"` | degree, then index of the left subtree, then index |
| `"Right"` | degree, then index of the right subtree, then index |
| `"PDegree"` | trees compared by P-degree, then index; contains a basis of the Lie algebra of classical mechanics (see below) |

The option `Reversed` fixes the bracket convention. With `Reversed -> True` (the default), a tree with left subtree t₁ and right subtree t₂ represents [t₂, t₁]. With `Reversed -> False` it represents [t₁, t₂].

**Representation of Lie series.** A Lie series truncated at degree N is stored as the vector of its coefficients in the Hall basis,

  A = Σ_{t ∈ H, |t| ≤ N}  h_t E_t ,

where E_t is the nested bracket of the Hall tree t. `HallCoefficients[A]` returns these coefficients h_t, in the order of `HallBasisElements[N]`. Brackets are computed with the structure constants of the Hall basis, [E_u, E_v] = Σ_s C^s_{uv} E_s, which are integers. `LieSeriesInitialize` computes them once, from the Lie coalgebra generated by the Hall trees in the Hopf algebra of bicolored rooted trees (Murua 2006; Casas and Murua 2009); the auxiliary trees used there are discarded afterwards. Users normally never build the vector by hand. Lie series are created from `LieSeries["X"]`, `LieSeries["Y"]`, `LieSeries[0]` and `GenericLieSeries`, and combined with the operations below.

## Installation

Requires Mathematica 12 or later (it may work on earlier versions, but has not been checked).

The simplest way is to install the paclet attached to the latest [GitHub release](https://github.com/Ander-Murua/LieSeries/releases) directly from Mathematica:

```mathematica
PacletInstall["https://github.com/Ander-Murua/LieSeries/releases/download/v1.0.0/LieSeries-1.0.0.paclet"];
Needs["LieSeries`"]
```

The installation is permanent: in later sessions, ``Needs["LieSeries`"]`` is enough.

Alternatively, download "Source code (zip)" from the assets of the [release](https://github.com/Ander-Murua/LieSeries/releases), unzip it (it gives a folder `LieSeries-1.0.0`), and either register the folder for the current session

```mathematica
PacletDirectoryLoad["/path/to/LieSeries-1.0.0"];
Needs["LieSeries`"]
```

or install it from the folder

```mathematica
PacletInstall[CreatePacletArchive["/path/to/LieSeries-1.0.0"]];
Needs["LieSeries`"]
```

or simply load the package file directly:

```mathematica
Get["/path/to/LieSeries-1.0.0/Kernel/LieSeries.wl"]
```

## Quick start

```mathematica
Needs["LieSeries`"]

LieSeriesInitialize[{"X", "Y"}, 5];          (* Hall basis up to degree 5 *)
X = LieSeries["X"]; Y = LieSeries["Y"];

A = X + LieBracket[X, Y]/2 + LieBracket[LieBracket[X, Y], X - t Y]
HallCoefficients[A, {3}]                      (* coefficients of degree 3 *)
D[A, t]                                       (* operations act on the coefficients *)

$BCHLieSeries                                 (* log(e^X e^Y) up to degree 5 *)
NonZeroHallCoefficients[$BCHLieSeries]

BCH[X + Y, -X]                                (* BCH of two Lie series *)
BCH[{{0, 1}, {0, 0}}, {{0, 0}, {1, 0}}, 4]    (* BCH of two matrices, terms up to degree 4 *)

LieSeriesInitialize[{"X", "Y"}, 6, HallOrder -> "Lyndon", Reversed -> False];
NonZeroHallCoefficients[$BCHLieSeries]       (* BCH in the Lyndon basis *)
```

The infix operator `\[Diamond]` (typed `Esc dia Esc`) is a shorthand for `LieBracket`: `X ⋄ Y`.

See `Examples/Introduction.nb` for a guided tour: generic series, substitution into matrices and vector fields, BCH, symmetric BCH, exp(ad_A)B, Zassenhaus terms, the Thompson problem, and the Lie algebra of classical mechanics.

## The Lie algebra of classical mechanics

The Lie algebra of classical mechanics (McLachlan & Murua 2019) is the quotient L(A,B)/I, where I is the Lie ideal generated by [B,[B,[B,A]]]. It models the Lie algebra generated by a kinetic energy A, quadratic in the momenta, and a potential energy B under the Poisson bracket.

To work in L(A,B)/I, initialize with `LieAlgebra -> "ClassicalMechanics"`:

```mathematica
LieSeriesInitialize[{"A", "B"}, 8, LieAlgebra -> "ClassicalMechanics"];
A = LieSeries["A"]; B = LieSeries["B"];
LieBracket[B, LieBracket[B, LieBracket[B, A]]]      (* displays 0 *)
HallDimension /@ Range[8]                            (* {2, 1, 2, 2, 4, 5, 10, 14} *)
$BCHLieSeries                                        (* BCH series in L(A,B)/I *)
```

In this mode, `HallBasisElements`, `HallCoefficients`, `NonZeroHallCoefficients`, `ZeroLieSeriesEquations`, `HallDimension`, `GenericLieSeries`, `LieSeriesMap` and the display of Lie series refer to L(A,B)/I. Internally, Lie series are still elements of L(A,B), so substitutions (`LieSeriesSubstitution`, `BCH` with matrices, …) are exact even for objects that do not satisfy [b,[b,[b,a]]] = 0.

The basis of L(A,B)/I comes from the generalized Hall basis `HallOrder -> "PDegree"` of L(A,B), built from the *P-degree*: 2 for A (the first generator), 0 for B, and max(p₁ + p₂ − 1, 0) for a bracket, i.e. the polynomial degree in the momenta. Trees are ordered by P-degree, then by index. The Hall elements in which no subtree is a bracket of two elements of P-degree 0 form a basis of L(A,B)/I, and the others span I. The tests check the dimensions against Table 5 of the paper up to degree 20.

`ClassicalMechanicsBasisElements` and `ClassicalMechanicsCoefficients` give this basis and the coefficients in it. They also work in the free Lie algebra with `HallOrder -> "PDegree"`, where they show which part of an element of L(A,B) survives in the quotient.

## Main functions

| Function | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `LieSeriesInitialize[{"X","Y"}, n, opts]` | set up the Hall basis up to degree n |
| `LieSeries["X"]`, `LieSeries[0]` | generators and zero |
| `LieBracket[A, B]`, `LieBracket[A, B, n]`, `LieBracket[A, B, {n}]` | bracket (all degrees, up to n, degree n) |
| `GenericLieSeries[a]`, `GenericLieSeries[a, n]` | series with symbolic Hall coefficients a[i] |
| `HallCoefficients[A]`, `NonZeroHallCoefficients[A]` | coefficients in the Hall basis |
| `HallBasisElements[n]`, `HallDimension[n]` | basis elements as nested brackets, dimensions |
| `ZeroLieSeriesEquations[A]` | equations for all coefficients of A to vanish |
| `LieSeriesSubstitution[A, {"X" -> a, "Y" -> b}]` | substitute generators (option `LieBracketFunction`) |
| `LieSeriesMap[f, A]` | apply f to each Hall coefficient of A |
| `LieSeriesProj[A, n]`, `LieSeriesProj[A, {n}]`, `LieSeriesSet[A, B, {n}]` | projections by degree |
| `BCH[A, B]`, `SymmetricBCH[A, B]`, `$BCHLieSeries`, `$SymmetricBCHLieSeries` | BCH series |
| `ExpAd[A, B]` | exp(ad_A) B |
| `ZassenhausTerms[n]` | Zassenhaus terms C₂, …, Cₙ |
| `ClassicalMechanicsBasisElements[n]`, `ClassicalMechanicsCoefficients[A]` | basis and coefficients in the Lie algebra of classical mechanics, in either mode |
| `HallOrders[]`, `LieSeriesBasisInfo[]` | available bases, description of the current basis |

Options of `LieSeriesInitialize`: `HallOrder`, `LieAlgebra` (`"Free"` or `"ClassicalMechanics"`), `Reversed`, `BCHLieSeries`, `SymmetricBCHLieSeries` (precompute the BCH series), `FormatMaxDegree`, `LieBracketFunction`, `SparseArrays`, and `CheckLessFunction` (check that the Hall order is a strict total order on the Hall trees). An unknown option gives the message `LieSeriesInitialize::optx` and `$Failed`.

## Differences from the original code

The original version of this package (`LieSeries.m`, 2007–2008, never released) was used to compute the BCH tables of Casas and Murua (2009). Notebooks written for it should run with minor changes:

- The package now lives in the context ``LieSeries` ``. Internal tables (`l`, `r`, `f`, `degree`, `sigma`, …) are private and no longer collide with user symbols.
- Lie series carry a stamp of the basis they were built in. After a new call to `LieSeriesInitialize`, old series are reported as invalid instead of being silently reinterpreted.
- The notation `{A, B}` for `[A, B]` and the option `CurlyBracketNotation` have been removed, because they also turned two-element lists of series (e.g. results of `Table`) into brackets. Use `LieBracket[A, B]` or `A ⋄ B`.
- The order is chosen with `HallOrder -> "Lyndon"` etc. The option `LessFunction` and the functions `LyndonLess`, `LessTall`, `LessLeft`, `LessRight` no longer exist. User-defined order functions are not supported; a new built-in order can be added as explained below.
- Lie series are stored by their Hall coefficients (the original code stored their values on all the trees generated by the algorithm). In particular, `f /@ A` applies `f` to the Hall coefficients of `A`. The option `GenericLieSeries` of `LieSeriesInitialize`, which precomputed the Hall basis elements, is no longer needed and has been removed (the function `GenericLieSeries` remains).
- `LieBracketFunction` is now also an option of `LieSeriesSubstitution`, `BCH` and `SymmetricBCH`.
- `LieSeriesSubstitution` accepts the rules in any order.
- Bug fixes are listed in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Tests

```mathematica
Needs["LieSeries`"];
TestReport["/path/to/LieSeries/Tests/LieSeries.wlt"]
```

The notebook `Tests/test.nb` does the same. Most of the 35 tests check mathematical identities rather than stored outputs. They cover:

- the dimensions against Witt's formula, for every built-in order;
- antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity;
- BCH, symmetric BCH, exp(ad) and Zassenhaus against exact matrix exponentials and logarithms of nilpotent matrices, for every built-in order;
- the bracket, BCH and symmetric BCH up to degree 12, against exact computations with 13×13 nilpotent integer matrices (several Hall orders, both values of `Reversed`), and a bracket of degree 11 computed independently;
- the stored structure constants (nonzero integers, stored only for u < v);
- the checks of the Hall orders: the built-in orders pass them, and an invalid order is detected;
- the inverse property of BCH;
- the published BCH and symmetric BCH tables of Casas and Murua (2009), up to degree 8, in both the Hall and the Lyndon basis. The files in `Tests/data` are the first 71 rows of data of the tables available at <https://www.ehu.eus/ccwmuura/bch.html>.

## For developers

### Adding a new built-in Hall basis

In `Kernel/LieSeries.wl`, write an order function `less[j, k]` on tree indices, using the private tables `l`, `r`, `degree`, `treeWord`, `tallDegree`, `pdegree`. Then add an entry to `$hallOrders` with a short description. Nothing else needs to change.

A strict total order `less` on bicolored rooted trees determines a unique decomposition t = l(t) ∘ r(t) of each tree t with more than one vertex: r(t) is the greatest (for `less`) of the subtrees attached to the root of t, and t is obtained by grafting r(t) onto the root of l(t). The Hall trees are X, Y and, recursively, the trees t such that l(t) and r(t) are Hall trees and `less[r(t), l(t)]`. They form a generalized Hall set (and the corresponding nested brackets a Hall–Viennot basis) provided that `less[r(t), t]` for every Hall tree t with more than one vertex, i.e. each Hall tree is greater than all its proper subtrees.

A convenient sufficient condition is that `less[r(t), t]` for *every* tree t with more than one vertex, which is equivalent to each tree being greater than all its proper subtrees. It holds, for instance, if `less` compares the degree first, and it is usually the easiest way to show that a new order gives a Hall set.

In the code, Hall trees are tree indices, l and r are the tables `l[t]`, `r[t]`, and `less[j, k]` is a function of two tree indices. Two requirements apply:

- (R1) `less` must be a strict total order on all trees, not only on Hall trees, because it also gives the normal form of the auxiliary trees used by `LieSeriesInitialize`; and
- (R2) `less[r[t], t]` for every Hall tree t with more than one vertex.

(R2) is always checked (message `LieSeriesInitialize::order3`); (R1) is checked on the Hall trees with `CheckLessFunction -> True`. For the definition of Hall sets see Reutenauer (1993, Ch. 4); for the representation of Hall sets by rooted trees, Murua (2006, Remark 17) and Casas and Murua (2009, Sec. II.C). Run the tests with the new order added to `$orders` in `Tests/LieSeries.wlt`.

### Timings

`Benchmarks/LieSeriesTimings.wl` times `LieSeriesInitialize` and the BCH series for given orders and degrees, records the time of each phase of the initialization (``LieSeries`Private`$buildTimings``), and compares versions of `Kernel/LieSeries.wl`. It also stores fingerprints of the structure constants and of the BCH coefficients, to check that a change of the code does not change the results. See the comments at the top of the file.
